Scene Builder

Erotic fantasies. Do you have them? How do you feel about them?

They can be sources of inspiration, mirrors of our deepest longings. And they can feel scary, shameful and confusing too.

I created this tool to help you understand your fantasies better, and to build toward a version you’d actually like to try in real life.

Whether you keep what you find to yourself or share it with someone is entirely up to you. A fantasy is not a request.

Before you begin, take a moment to create a space where you can sink into your imagination: soft light, somewhere cozy and private. Get yourself a nice drink, get comfortable, and begin.

I wish you a wonderful journey.

Sources and inspiration

The steps in this tool draw on the work of others:

  • Jack Morin, The Erotic Mind: the peak erotic experience exercise, the four cornerstones of eroticism, and the idea that attraction plus an obstacle creates excitement.
  • Justin Lehmiller, Tell Me What You Want: the seven most common fantasy themes, from his survey of over 4,000 people.
  • Emily Nagoski, Come as You Are: the dual control model of accelerators and brakes.
  • Richard Schwartz, Internal Family Systems: listening to the different parts of you.
  • Betty Martin, the Wheel of Consent: who it’s for and who’s doing.
  • The kink community’s practice of scene negotiation: limits, signals, check-ins and aftercare.

Everything you write stays in this browser on this device. Nothing is uploaded, and the page isn’t listed in search engines. Anyone using this browser can open it, so mind shared devices. Download a backup now and then, and use it to move your scenes to another device.
